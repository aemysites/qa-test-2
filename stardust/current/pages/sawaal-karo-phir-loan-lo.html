and the answers you should know before borrowing.

What is the interest rate on a personal loan?

Personal loan interest rates vary based on your credit score, income, and lender. Always ask for the exact rate applicable to you, not just the “starting from” rate.

What will be my total repayment amount?

Your total repayment includes principal + interest + fees. Ask for a breakup so you know how much extra you’ll pay over the loan amount.

Are there any processing or hidden charges?

Most personal loans have processing fees and other charges. Always confirm all fees in writing before accepting the loan.

What happens if I miss an EMI?

Missing an EMI can lead to penalty charges and a drop in your credit score. Repeated delays can make future loans expensive or difficult.

Can I repay the loan early?

Some lenders allow prepayment but charge a foreclosure penalty. Ask when prepayment is allowed and at what cost.

Are there charges for EMI bounce or cheque return?

EMI bounces usually attract penalties and negatively impact your credit score.

Will I receive a loan statement and repayment schedule?

Always ask for a detailed amortization schedule and periodic statements.

Is the interest rate fixed or floating?

Fixed rates stay the same, while floating rates change with the market. Floating rates can go up or down, affecting your EMI.

How often does the floating interest rate change?

Floating rates are usually linked to RBI benchmarks and may change multiple times during the loan tenure.

What is the total cost of the home loan?

Over long tenures, interest can be much higher than the loan amount. Always ask for the total payable amount.

Are there prepayment or foreclosure charges?

Many home loans allow prepayment without charges, but this depends on the lender and loan type.

What happens if I face financial difficulty in future?

Ask about options like EMI restructuring, tenure extension, or temporary relief.

Why is RERA registration important for a home buyer?

RERA ensures transparency, timely possession, and legal protection. It also allows buyers to raise complaints if the builder delays or changes plans.

Is my loan amount disbursed fully or in stages?

For under-construction properties, loans are usually disbursed in stages based on construction progress.

Is the business loan secured or unsecured?

Unsecured loans don’t need collateral but usually have higher interest rates.

How is the interest calculated?

Interest may be calculated on a monthly reducing balance or flat basis, which affects the total cost significantly.

What is the repayment frequency?

Some business loans require weekly or daily EMIs, which can impact cash flow.

What happens if my business income fluctuates?

Ask if the lender offers flexible EMIs or restructuring options during slow business periods.

Will my personal assets be at risk?

In some cases, lenders may ask for a personal guarantee, putting personal assets at risk.

What is the minimum business vintage required?

Some lenders require the business to be operational for a minimum number of years.

Is the loan amount linked to my turnover or profit?

Loan limits are often based on business revenue, profitability, and bank transactions.

Can I use the loan for any business purpose?

Some loans restrict usage to specific purposes like inventory or machinery.

How much loan can I get against my property?

Lenders usually offer 50–70% of the property’s market value.

What happens if I fail to repay the loan?

Since the loan is secured, the lender has the right to take possession of the property.

Is the interest rate lower than a personal loan?

Yes, LAP rates are generally lower than personal loans but higher than home loans.

Are there any additional legal or valuation charges?

Yes, lenders usually charge property valuation and legal verification fees.

Can I prepay or foreclose the loan early?

Most lenders allow this, but some may charge a penalty.

Will I lose ownership of my property?

You retain ownership, but the property is mortgaged until the loan is fully repaid.

What happens if I am unable to repay the loan?

The lender has the right to recover dues by selling the mortgaged property.

Is the interest rate higher for a used car loan?

Yes, used car loans generally have higher interest rates than new car loans.

How is the car’s value decided?

The lender evaluates the car’s age, condition, and resale value.

Who owns the car during the loan tenure?

The car is hypothecated to the lender until the loan is fully repaid.

Are there prepayment charges?

Some lenders charge penalties for early repayment. Always confirm this upfront.

What if the car gets damaged or totalled?

Insurance plays a key role. Ask whether comprehensive insurance is mandatory.

What is the interest rate compared to a new car loan?

Used car loans generally have higher interest rates.

Is insurance mandatory for the loan?

Comprehensive insurance is usually required throughout the loan tenure.

What is the actual cost of the loan?

Apart from EMIs, there may be processing fees, insurance, and documentation charges.

Are zero down payment offers really free?

Often, the cost is adjusted through higher interest or longer tenure.

What happens if I miss an EMI?

Late payments attract penalties and affect your credit score.

Can I close the loan early?

Prepayment may be allowed after a few months, sometimes with a fee.

Who keeps the RC of the vehicle?

The RC is usually marked with lender hypothecation until loan closure.

Is the EMI calculated on a flat rate or reducing balance?

Flat rates appear cheaper but usually cost more overall than reducing balance interest.

Is a down payment mandatory?

Most lenders require a minimum down payment; confirm the exact amount

When do I have to start repaying the loan?

Repayment usually starts after course completion, following a moratorium period.

Is interest charged during the study period?

Yes, interest accrues during the study period and may be added to the loan amount.

Do my parents need to be co-borrowers?

In most cases, parents or guardians are mandatory co-borrowers.

Are there tax benefits on education loans?

Yes, interest paid on education loans may be eligible for tax deduction under applicable laws.

What happens if I don’t get a job immediately?

Ask the lender about repayment flexibility or extension options.

Are scholarships or grants adjusted against the loan amount?

Inform the lender if you receive scholarships—they may reduce the loan amount.

What happens if I pursue higher studies after completion?

Ask if the moratorium can be extended for further education.

Which securities can be pledged for LAS?

Most lenders accept equity shares, mutual funds, bonds, ETFs, and some insurance policies, subject to eligibility and lender policies.

How much loan can I get against my securities?

The loan amount depends on the type of security. Typically, lenders offer 50–70% of the value, also known as the Loan-to-Value (LTV) ratio.

What interest rate is charged on LAS?

LAS interest rates are generally lower than personal loans because the loan is secured. The exact rate depends on the type of security and market conditions.

Will I lose ownership of my investments?

No. You remain the owner, but the securities are pledged with the lender until the loan is repaid.

Can I continue to earn returns on my investments?

Yes. You may continue to earn dividends, interest, or bonuses, but selling or switching investments is restricted until loan closure.

What happens if the market value of my securities falls?

If the value drops sharply, the lender may ask for additional collateral or partial repayment. This is called a margin call.

What is a margin call and why is it important?

A margin call is a request by the lender to maintain the required security value. Ignoring it can lead to forced selling of your investments.

Does Your Lender Check All The Boxes?

The right lender makes all the difference. Use this checklist to verify your lender, 

spot red flags, and identify potential loan fraud before you borrow.

Lender Checklist

RBI Registration - Always check if the lender is registered with the RBI. If the lender is not on the official RBI list, do not proceed.

Official Website & Contact Details - A genuine lender has a secure (HTTPS) website and verified office address. Missing or unclear contact details are a major warning sign.

Unrealistic Loan Promises - Be cautious of instant approvals, no-documentation loans, or very low interest rates. Real lenders always conduct proper eligibility and credit checks.

 

Upfront Payment Demand - Legitimate lenders never ask for fees before loan approval. Any upfront payment request is a strong sign of fraud.

Communication from Official Channels - Loan communication should come from official emails or verified accounts. Personal numbers or free email IDs usually indicate scams.

Pressure to Act Immediately - Fraudsters use urgency to force quick decisions. Genuine lenders give you time to review and decide.

Excessive Phone or App Permissions - Lenders do not need access to contacts, photos, or social media. If an app asks for intrusive permissions, uninstall it immediately.

Online Reviews & Complaints - Always check reviews on app stores and social platforms. Repeated complaints about fraud or harassment are a red flag.

Clear & Transparent Loan Agreement - A valid loan must have a written agreement with all charges clearly stated. Never proceed if terms are unclear or undocumented.

Secure App or Website - Download loan apps only from official app stores. Avoid APK files, WhatsApp links, or unknown downloads.

Verified Social Media Presence - Credible lenders usually have verified social media pages. No digital footprint or complaint channel calls for caution.

Proper Sanction Letter - A sanction letter should clearly mention loan amount, interest, and charges. Refusal to share official documents is a serious warning.

Confidential Banking Details - No lender will ever ask for OTPs, PINs, or passwords. Sharing these details can lead to financial loss.

Grievance Redressal System - A genuine lender provides a clear complaint and escalation process. Absence of a grievance mechanism means you should avoid the lender.
